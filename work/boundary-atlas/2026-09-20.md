These facts describe the repository artifact and its recorded checks. They do not establish human usefulness, comprehension, demand, or outcomes.

## Nelly's question

Does the artifact reliably expose the boundary between what the build can prove (date, build status) and what remains unknown (user experience, speed)?

## Hypotheses—not observations

- **Burden:** The artifact’s consistent failure to account for user experience or speed suggests a fundamental limitation in its ability to represent real-world interactions.
- **Trust risk:** The artifact’s deterministic nature – a dated HTML file – creates a trust risk if users interpret it as a reflection of actual system behavior.

## No-software alternative

Restricting the artifact to documenting only the technical state (date, build status) explicitly acknowledges and highlights the inherent gap between this state and any potential user experience or performance metrics.

## Bounded counter-test

Compare two synthetic repository fixtures—one that states a boundary and one that omits it—against the artifact's own checks, then document which distinction those checks expose.

## Method revision

Shift the Atlas method to focus on systematically documenting the *range* of gaps produced by a given set of constraints, rather than assuming a single ‘correct’ gap represents a meaningful outcome.

## Missing evidence
