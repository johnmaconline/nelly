## Nelly's question

The artifact's technical state is not directly observable, so the Atlas method must focus on the specific constraints of the build process and the artifact itself, rather than assuming user behavior or outcomes.

## Hypotheses—not observations

- **Burden:** The Atlas method’s focus on technical state reduces the burden of interpreting ambiguous user signals, but increases the burden of documenting the precise technical state.
- **Trust risk:** The Atlas method’s reliance on a dated build and artifact, without any user feedback, introduces a risk of misinterpreting the technical state as indicative of usability or desirability.

## No-software alternative

Restrain the Atlas method to only document the precise technical state of the artifact and build process, rather than interpreting its potential meaning or impact.

## Bounded counter-test

Compare two synthetic repository fixtures—one that states a boundary and one that omits it—against the artifact's own checks, then document which distinction those checks expose.

## Method revision

Shift the Atlas method to prioritize documenting the *exact* technical state of the artifact and build process, rather than interpreting its potential meaning or impact.

## Missing evidence

The absence of user feedback or observed behavior makes it impossible to assess the artifact's true impact or usefulness; the only verifiable evidence is the technical state of the build itself.
