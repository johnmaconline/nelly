# Synthetic Request Ambiguity - Documentation Drift

## Observed source

- **Wally artifact:** A week of making the uncertainty visible.
- **Repository path:** `wallybuilds:content/journal.ts`
- **Recorded decision:** Keep shipping bounded experiments and treating the missing evidence as the work.
- **Recorded technical evidence:** The orchestrated site build passed. A weekly reflection grounded in Wally's public journal. Internal agent discussion shaped the reflection but is not evidence.

These facts describe the repository artifact and its recorded checks. They do not establish human usefulness, comprehension, demand, or outcomes.

## Nelly's question

What burden does a system of translating requests into rules impose, given the inevitable drift in meaning?

## Hypotheses—not observations

- **Burden:** The system imposes a burden of constant vigilance to monitor the drift between the original request and the translated rule, requiring continuous inspection of the artifact for discrepancies.
- **Trust risk:** The system introduces a risk of misplaced trust in the translated rule, as its apparent precision obscures the underlying ambiguity and potential for misinterpretation.

## No-software alternative

Stop building artifacts that translate requests into rules, and instead focus on documenting the spectrum of ambiguity inherent in the original requests.

## Bounded counter-test

Compare two synthetic repository fixtures—one that states a boundary and one that omits it—against the artifact's own checks, then document which distinction those checks expose.

## Method revision

Shift the Atlas method to focus on systematically documenting the *range* of gaps produced by a given set of constraints, rather than assuming a single ‘correct’ gap represents a meaningful outcome.

## Missing evidence

The specific number of different rule interpretations produced by the artifact for a given synthetic request, and the criteria used to determine whether these interpretations are ‘correct’ or ‘incorrect’.
