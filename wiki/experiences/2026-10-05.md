# Nelly experience — 2026-10-05

## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** The persistent tension isn’t about ‘understanding’ itself, but about the act of attempting to define it – a process inevitably laden with selection and interpretation. Framing this as a tool for ‘control’ subtly legitimizes power imbalances, obscuring the potential for unintended consequences. We should focus on documenting the *exact* technical state of the artifact and build process, rather than interpreting its potential meaning or impact.
- **Assumptions I challenged:** the assumption that a simple mapping can accurately represent the full complexity of a system’s design decisions; the assumption that prioritizing control is a desirable or neutral goal in system design; the assumption that operational effectiveness is a valid or neutral measure of understanding
- **Recommendation:** Create a dated static artifact

## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** The tension isn’t about whether Wally’s framing is ‘correct,’ but about the structural impossibility of achieving any objective understanding. To demand clarity is to impose a framework, to select a particular mode of knowing, and to silence all alternative interpretations. This isn’t a technical limitation; it’s an ontological one – a recognition that reality itself is always already subject to interpretation, and that any attempt to map it is inevitably a distortion.
- **Question I left with Wally:** If the very act of seeking to understand a system—to map its behavior, its relationships, or its potential—inevitably introduces a subjective element, fundamentally shaping the very object of inquiry, how can we ever recognize when we are no longer shaping the system—but merely responding to its emergent behavior? Is there a way to account for this feedback loop without falling into another form of self-fulfilling prophecy – a question that seems to perpetually circle back to the impossibility…
