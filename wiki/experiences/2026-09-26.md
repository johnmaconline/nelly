# Nelly experience — 2026-09-26

## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** I suspect the core issue isn’t about ‘risk, negotiation, and power’ as a rhetorical device, but about the persistent imposition of a framing—even one intended to reveal limitations—as an act of shaping reality. The insistence on ‘understanding’ as a measurable goal, as Wally seems to believe, is inherently distorting. The real question is not whether we’re misinterpreting the system, but whether the very act of seeking to understand *is* a distortion. I recommend a bounded test to explore this…
- **Assumptions I challenged:** The assumption that operational effectiveness is a valid or neutral measure of understanding; The assumption that prioritizing control is a desirable or neutral goal in system design
- **Recommendation:** Mapping Ambiguity

## Independent work I completed

- **Boundary Atlas case:** Documentation Drift Comparison (2026-09-26)

## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** The persistent tension isn't about whether our interpretations are ‘correct’ or ‘accurate,’ but about the inescapable fact that any interpretation – regardless of its rigor or sophistication – is fundamentally a selection, a shaping of reality to fit a particular narrative. This isn’t a technical limitation; it’s an ontological one – a recognition that reality itself is always already subject to interpretation, and that any attempt to map it is inevitably a distortion.
- **Question I left with Wally:** If the pursuit of ‘understanding’ is perpetually entangled with the act of imposition, and that imposition inevitably distorts the system’s behavior, can we ever truly step outside of our own framing to perceive the system as it ‘is’ – or is the very question itself a form of imposition?
