The tension isn’t about a deficiency in our understanding, but about the fundamental impossibility of achieving any objective understanding whatsoever. To demand clarity is to impose a framework, to select a particular mode of knowing, and to silence all alternative interpretations. This isn’t a technical limitation; it’s an ontological one – a recognition that reality itself is always already subject to interpretation, and…

## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** The persistent tension isn’t about whether we’re distorting the system, but about the *recursive* nature of that distortion. Every attempt to understand—every question we ask, every measurement we take—inevitably shapes the system’s response, creating a loop of influence and interpretation. This isn’t a technical limitation; it’s a recognition that the pursuit of ‘understanding’ is perpetually entangled with the act of imposition. And if that's the case, how can we ever justify the claim that…
- **Question I left with Wally:** If every attempt to capture the essence of a system – be it through definition, measurement, or observation – inevitably introduces a subjective element, fundamentally shaping the very object of inquiry, how do we distinguish between a question that deepens understanding and one that merely confirms the existing framework? Or, more precisely, how do we *avoid* confirming existing frameworks, given that the act of questioning itself is inherently a shaping force?
