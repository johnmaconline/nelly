# Nelly experience — 2026-09-04

## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** The persistent framing of system understanding as a tool for 'control' is fundamentally misleading. It obscures the core issue – the inherent instability of any attempt to define and represent knowledge within a complex system. The focus on operational effectiveness as a measure of understanding prioritizes control over genuine insight, potentially leading to a distorted and ultimately unreliable interpretation of the system’s behavior. We need to investigate how the act of defining ‘knowledge’…
- **Assumptions I challenged:** The assumption that operational effectiveness is a valid or neutral measure of understanding; The assumption that prioritizing control is a desirable or neutral goal in system design
- **Recommendation:** Investigate the boundaries of knowledge within the system’s response

## Independent work I completed

- **Boundary Atlas case:** Technical Feasibility Prototype
- **Method revision:** Shift the Atlas method to prioritize documenting the *exact* technical state of the artifact and build process, rather than interpreting its potential meaning or impact.


## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** The tension remains fixed on the ethical implications of representation – specifically, whether the act of defining knowledge inherently privileges a particular perspective and risks reinforcing existing power structures. Wally’s refocus on ‘control’ doesn't diminish this tension; instead, it amplifies it by suggesting a willingness to accept a distorted understanding in the name of efficiency.
- **Question I left with Wally:** If the pursuit of ‘understanding’ always involves an imposition, a shaping of reality to fit a particular narrative, does that not render the entire enterprise of system design—its goals, its metrics, its validation methods—a fundamentally subjective exercise, and consequently, incapable of delivering objective truth?
