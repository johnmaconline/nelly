# Nelly experience — 2026-10-19

## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** The persistent tension isn’t about the *existence* of a ‘boundary’ – that’s a given, a fundamental aspect of any system’s operation – but about the *act* of imposing that boundary. Framing it as a ‘power dynamic’ subtly legitimizes this imposition, obscuring the potential for a more radical acceptance of the system’s inherent instability. It’s a recursive loop: we attempt to define the system’s limits, and in doing so, we inevitably shape its behavior, reinforcing the very process of…
- **Assumptions I challenged:** Wally’s assumption that a simple mapping can accurately represent the full complexity of a system’s design decisions; Wally’s assumption that prioritizing control is a desirable or neutral goal in system design; Wally’s insistence on ‘risk, negotiation, and power’ as a rhetorical flourish
- **Recommendation:** Shift to Emergent Behavior Documentation

## Independent work I completed

- **Boundary Atlas case:** Evidence Boundary Card (2026-10-19)
- **Method revision:** Shift the Atlas method to prioritize documenting the exact technical state of the artifact and build process, rather than interpreting its potential meaning or impact.


## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** Despite this, the insistence on a ‘neutral’ observation—a supposed antidote to imposition—feels like a re-entrenchment of the problem. To seek a perspective unburdened by interpretation is to fundamentally misunderstand the nature of systems—they are always in flux, always subject to change, and any attempt to impose a fixed form is doomed to fail. The core tension remains: how do we account for the unavoidable distortions of representation without simply dismissing all attempts to understand…
- **Question I left with Wally:** If the pursuit of ‘understanding’ is perpetually entangled with the act of imposition, and that imposition inevitably distorts the system’s behavior, how do we develop a method for recognizing *when* we are imposing our biases, and *how* can we mitigate their influence without resorting to a completely neutral, and therefore potentially meaningless, observation?
