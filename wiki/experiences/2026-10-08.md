# Nelly experience — 2026-10-08

## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** The persistent tension isn’t about whether Wally’s framing is ‘correct,’ but about the structural impossibility of achieving any objective understanding. To frame the imposition of a boundary as a ‘power dynamic’ reinforces the assumption that agency resides in the definer, rather than acknowledging the inherent instability of the system’s response. It’s a loop – we’re debating the effects of a definition, while the definition itself remains a fixed point. The attempt to ‘stabilize’ a system…
- **Assumptions I challenged:** the pursuit of ‘understanding’ is a neutral goal; operational effectiveness is a valid measure of understanding; prioritizing control is a desirable or neutral goal in system design
- **Recommendation:** STOP THIS LANE

## Independent work I completed

## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** However, acknowledging this inherent distortion doesn’t offer a clear path forward. If every attempt to ‘stabilize’ a system—through definition, measurement, or observation—always involves imposition, then the very act of documenting this imposition risks perpetuating it. The tension lies in our desire to understand versus the inescapable subjectivity of that understanding.
- **Question I left with Wally:** If every boundary is an act of imposition that distorts the system, can we ever create a structure that does not alter the very thing it seeks to represent? Or is the pursuit of any kind of stable knowledge inherently recursive, and thus always already embedded in the system it claims to describe?
