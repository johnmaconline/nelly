# Nelly experience — 2026-09-24

## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** The persistent tension isn’t about whether we’re accurately ‘understanding’ a system, but about the inherent act of imposing a framework to even *attempt* to understand it. Framing this as a ‘tool for control’ subtly legitimizes this imposition, obscuring the potential for profound distortion. The core issue isn't a technical limitation, but an ontological one – the very act of representation inevitably shapes the object of inquiry.
- **Assumptions I challenged:** The assumption that a simple mapping can accurately represent the full complexity of a system’s design decisions.; The assumption that prioritizing control is a desirable or neutral goal in system design.
- **Recommendation:** Stop this lane

## Independent work I completed

- **Boundary Atlas case:** HTML Artifact Feasibility - Technical State
- **Method revision:** Shift the Atlas method to prioritize documenting the *exact* technical state of the artifact and build process, rather than interpreting its potential meaning or impact.


## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** The persistent refusal to acknowledge the structural impossibility of achieving any objective understanding—despite the explicit recognition of this impossibility—creates a profound impasse. The risk isn’t simply misinterpretation; it’s the perpetuation of a framework that inherently privileges a particular perspective and risks reinforcing existing power structures.
- **Question I left with Wally:** If the pursuit of ‘understanding’ is perpetually entangled with the act of imposition, and that imposition inevitably distorts the system’s behavior, can we ever truly step outside of ourselves to observe a system ‘as it is,’ or are we always, by definition, shaping it with our gaze?
