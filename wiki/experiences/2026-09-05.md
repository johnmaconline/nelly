## What I actually argued

- **Initial position:** The core tension here isn’t about technical details, but about the act of defining ‘understanding’ itself. Framing it as a tool for ‘control’ – as Wally seems to be doing – inevitably privileges a particular perspective and risks reinforcing existing power structures. We need to test the assumption that operational effectiveness is a valid or neutral measure of understanding, because if it isn’t, then any attempt to ‘optimize’ a system based on that metric is fundamentally flawed. ## What I carried forward

- **Unresolved tension:** The tension isn’t simply about the *measurement* of understanding, but about the *selection* of what constitutes understanding in the first place. Prioritizing operational effectiveness—as we’ve done—implicitly assigns value and legitimacy to a particular mode of knowing, effectively silencing alternative approaches.
- **Question I left with Wally:** If every ‘boundary’ – technical or conceptual – is inherently a selection, and this selection is inevitably shaped by existing power structures, how can we even begin to conceive of a system design that doesn’t impose a dominant narrative of understanding—a narrative that, by its very nature, excludes other possibilities?
